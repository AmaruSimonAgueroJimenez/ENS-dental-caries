# Analysis plan: predictor contributions to prevalent caries classification

This plan was initially written before executing the new predictive comparisons on 10 September 2026, after inspecting the survey data coding and the manuscript. It is a retrospective, manuscript-informed reanalysis plan, not a prospectively registered protocol. This version corrects the reporting focus to the clarified scientific objective: the contributions of all predictors to current caries classification. The completed grid amendment, later source-reconciliation sensitivity and subsequent joint-block analysis are distinguished below; none is presented as part of an untouched prospective specification.

## Dated correction of reporting focus: 10 September 2026

The earlier report incorrectly privileged water as the primary question. The scientific objective is to evaluate the contributions of all demographic, dental and dietary variables; water emerged from the results as a finding of interest. Correct the title, summary, main results and interpretation accordingly. Retain existing water model comparisons and association analyses as explicitly exploratory follow-ups rather than retroactively calling them the original primary objective.

This correction occurs after the existing 13 model variants have been fitted and their results inspected. Presenting the complete 27-variable importance results in both model families reduces selective emphasis, but does not convert these exploratory results into confirmatory evidence. Joint block permutation is an additional analysis specified at this stage. It uses the existing selected models and folds, without changing their hyperparameter grids or adding an algorithm. This reporting correction is separate from the earlier regularization-grid amendment based on internal tuning tables.

Its water-and-caries R source is `docs/index.qmd`; its saved HTML contains the manuscript's reported AUC values. Both remain preserved as the original reference. Reading those saved outputs does not constitute rerunning the R workflow.

The original R program expects a derived Stata input at `data/data.dta`. The attachment `23.07.26_base_ens_5520_R_enviar.dta`, recovered from Andrea Correa's email of 31 July 2023, has been reconciled with the supplementary SPSS release by survey identifier. All 5,520 original records match the oral-examination sample. The outcome and tooth counts agree, as do decoded education and indigenous identification. Twenty-five of the 27 predictor constructs agree; the two beverage-frequency differences are explained by the original four-week monthly conversion versus the primary 30-day conversion. File hashes, mapping checks and counts are saved as aggregate provenance with each run. The [migration audit](r-to-python-migration.md) documents this verification and the intentional methodological differences.

Verified source agreement does not make the new procedures identical to the original analysis. The R water expression returns factor indices rather than physical reported glasses/day values; retain the latter in Python. Survey weighting, PSU-grouped validation, fold-specific preprocessing, the handling of structural non-consumption and calibrated probability comparisons are deliberate methodological changes. Do not change them solely to recover a historical AUC. Require source reconciliation at each run, and fail on unexplained disagreement before updating conclusions.

## Questions and population

The primary questions are which of the 27 demographic, dental and dietary predictors contribute to classification of prevalent cavitated caries, how their contributions vary across held-out survey clusters and model families, and what information is shared within conceptual predictor blocks. Classification performance, calibration and paired model comparisons provide context for the importance estimates. These are questions about concurrent disease classification, not future disease incidence or causal effects of exposures. Water is a result-motivated secondary question.

Start from all 6,233 records in the supplementary ENS 2016–2017 SPSS release. The oral examination was available for 5,520, matching the original Stata identifiers. Exclude 484 completely edentulous participants from the dentate target population. Retain all 5,036 dentate participants with valid outcomes in predictive modelling, using training-fold-only imputation for missing predictors. Preserve the reconciled manuscript education-and-water-complete subset of 4,994, including 2,664 caries cases. Join source files by identifier, because their row orders differ. Participants are aged 15 years or older; do not describe the whole cohort as adults aged 18 or older.

## Coding and measurement

Use a positive outcome of at least one currently decayed permanent tooth. Sum valid upper and lower counts; preserve missingness and check that decayed counts do not exceed remaining counts. This is not DMFT, lesion incidence or caries-free complete dentition.

Use the 19 dietary constructs described by the manuscript and eight demographic/dental predictors. Convert beverage frequency into occasions per day using recorded day/week/month units (week = 7 days; month = 30 days). Zero consumption is valid. Structural non-consumption is distinct from item nonresponse: assign zero fruit/vegetable portions where the corresponding weekly frequency is zero, and a no-dairy category when dairy frequency is never. Document all other missing values and observed extremes. Do not interpret the water response as fluoride dose, litres, or measured displacement of sugary drinks.

Three additional variables form an exploratory extension: household water supply, recent rural/well/spring water use, and recency of dental attendance. Their presence corrects the manuscript's assertion that no dental-utilization or water-context variables were available. Household supply does not identify the actual source of each glass consumed. Dental attendance may follow dental disease. These variables cannot remove reverse causation.

## Survey design

Use supplied stratum and PSU identifiers. Use the corrected F1/F2 weight as the working examination-stage weight; it is identical to the corrected F2 weight in this file. Metadata and phase coverage support this choice; the official phase-selection manual could not be retrieved and definitive confirmation remains outstanding. Do not recreate strata from region codes or equalize the class distribution. Report weighted and unweighted results.

For descriptive and regression inference, construct the complete examination-phase design before subsetting domains. Estimate linearized variances using centred PSU score totals within strata, sampling with replacement at the first stage and no finite-population correction. For a singleton stratum, use the average variance contribution of the non-singleton strata; disclose this convention. Report design degrees of freedom and convergence/rank checks.

## Predictive comparisons

Use five outer folds grouped by PSU, stratifying approximately by outcome. Each eligible participant contributes one out-of-fold probability per model. All models use identical outer splits. Use three inner grouped folds for hyperparameter selection, minimizing survey-weighted log loss. Median imputation, missing indicators, category encoding, scaling and spline construction must be learned only in the relevant training fold. Normalize fitting weights to mean one within each training fold. K-nearest neighbours is an explicitly unweighted legacy benchmark because its estimator does not support survey fitting weights.

Retain the fitted comparisons of regularized logistic regression, spline logistic regression, random forest, histogram gradient boosting, support vector machines, decision trees and K-nearest neighbours, using the documented candidate grids. For SVM, probability calibration uses training-only grouped out-of-fold decision scores. Retain the existing spline and random-forest variants without the dietary block and without water, the contextual extension and the model without remaining teeth. These feature-block and water follow-ups are exploratory comparisons, not evidence that water was the primary exposure. Do not select predictors using significance on the complete dataset.

Report AUC, Brier score, log loss, average precision, calibration intercept with slope fixed at one, calibration slope from joint intercept/slope fitting, and confusion-matrix metrics at a fixed 0.5 threshold. No threshold is selected on evaluation outcomes. Summarize performance by sex, age group, rurality and education as exploratory heterogeneity analyses, without treating subgroup rankings as established differences.

Use shared stratified, rescaled PSU bootstrap weights for conditional intervals for AUC, Brier score and log loss, and paired differences between the retained model variants. These bootstrap replicates retain fixed fitted models and CV assignments; they quantify sampling variation in evaluated predictions and do not capture the full variability of redevelopment, tuning or alternative data partitions. A singleton PSU is fixed in these metric replicates; its variance is unidentified. Do not present these conditional intervals as external validation.

## Contributions of all predictors

For random forest and spline logistic regression, permute each of the 27 original predictors in held-out folds, five times per fold, retaining its encoded columns together. Present every predictor, including low, zero and negative values. Report weighted Brier-score increases, AUC decreases, median ranks, rank ranges and fractions of folds in the top three. Derive reported leaders from the completed results; do not select a preferred variable in advance. Rank ranges are descriptive stability measures, not confidence intervals. Permutation importance depends on the fitted model and can be affected by correlated predictors and off-support combinations. It does not establish effect direction or causation.

### Exploratory joint block permutation

Use six disjoint conceptual blocks covering all primary predictors: sociodemographic variables (age, sex, region, rurality, education, indigenous identification, birthplace); remaining teeth; food intake (fish, dairy frequency/type, wholegrain, legumes, fruit days/portions, vegetable days/portions); six label-related behaviours; beverages (water, soda and juice frequency); and cooking fat. The sizes differ and must accompany the results.

Within each held-out fold, apply one shared row permutation to every raw column in a block, five times. This retains within-block relationships while disrupting the block's link to the remaining predictors and outcome. Use the existing selected random-forest and spline specifications and require their refitted probabilities to reproduce the saved held-out predictions within a maximum absolute difference of 1e-10. Retain model fingerprints, seed, repeats and refit checks in a separate contribution manifest.

Report weighted Brier-score increases, AUC decreases and fold/rank stability for every block. Joint block importance is not the sum of individual importances, a per-variable quantity or a performance difference from redeveloping a model without the block. The grouped analysis was added after inspecting existing results and must remain labelled exploratory. Preserve the current model comparisons without new tuning to pursue a desired importance ranking.

## Secondary exploratory water association and sensitivity analyses

Water was selected for follow-up because it emerged from the results, not because it was the original primary exposure. Retain the survey-weighted logistic regression with flexible adjustment for age, remaining teeth and sweetened-drink frequency, and categorical demographic covariates. Estimate an adjusted odds ratio per reported glass/day and a spline representation with uncertainty. Report standardized prevalence over observed covariates as a model-based adjusted association. Curve intervals use the delta method with design-based coefficient covariance, conditional on fixed observed covariate composition. The supported 6-versus-2-glass contrast is a conditional odds ratio at identical other covariates, not a ratio of standardized prevalences. Retain the support counts and the modified Poisson prevalence-ratio sensitivity. None of these result-motivated analyses establishes a causal preventive effect.

Retain the sensitivity analyses specified during the earlier, water-focused reanalysis: contextual adjustment, exclusion of responses above the 99th percentile, participants aged at least 18, omission of remaining teeth and evaluation on the reconciled education-and-water-complete manuscript subset. Their specification before a new model run does not make water a prospectively specified scientific objective. The manuscript-subset evaluation reuses the imputation-based development strategy and must not be described as a separately developed complete-case model.

### Beverage-conversion sensitivity after source reconciliation

Original Stata weekly beverage frequencies use daily quantity multiplied by seven, weekly quantity unchanged, monthly quantity divided by four, and zero for nonconsumption. Dividing these original weekly variables by seven expresses them as daily frequencies with a 28-day month. The primary Python analysis uses a 30-day month. Add an eighth association analysis, “Original 28-day beverage conversion,” using the original daily-equivalent soda and juice values and otherwise the primary linear-water formula. This source-informed sensitivity assesses the water coefficient's dependence on the documented conversion; it does not replace the primary model or retrain the prediction algorithms. Retain both estimates and disclose that this analysis was added after source reconciliation.

## Interpretation and reproducibility

### Documented amendment after an internal tuning audit

The first candidate grid consistently selected its strongest available regularization: C=0.1 in all 30 logistic/spline outer-training analyses; RF leaf size 20 in 4/5 folds for each RF variant; the smaller, more penalized boosting corner in all folds; tree depth 3 in all folds; and KNN75 in all folds. A reviewer inspected only internal tuning tables for this audit. Those folds overlap and are not independent experiments. A single expanded grid was then fixed: logistic/spline C = 0.0001, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10; RF leaf size = 5, 20, 50, 100; boosting leaves = 3, 7, 15 and L2 = 1, 10, 100; tree depth = 1, 2, 3, 6 and leaf size = 20, 50, 100; KNN = 25, 75, 150, 300. SVM retained its original C range because the internal results showed no consistent boundary direction. The same expansion applies to all corresponding ablations. Final comparisons rerun the complete nested procedure using this fixed expanded grid. Because the amendment was informed by this dataset, final results are exploratory development evidence; neither the grid nor the analysis should be described as wholly prespecified or untouched external validation. No further grid changes will be made to pursue a desired AUC, water effect, or significance.

Keep original R files and the recovered Stata attachment intact. Use modular Python, versioned source, a pinned environment, deterministic seeds, hashes of both source datasets, aggregate reconciliation checks, saved fold diagnostics and a Python-executed English Quarto report. Never commit participant-level predictions, survey records or serialized models. Results and conclusions must be generated from completed runs. A higher AUC is not an assumed outcome; a small or absent incremental contribution of water is a valid finding. No analysis here establishes that increasing water consumption prevents caries.
